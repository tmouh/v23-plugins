# Industrial Forensics

Industrial has been the darling of CRE for the past several years. By 2026, that has cooled — supply caught up in many markets and tenant demand normalized post-COVID-bull-whip. Apply the same skepticism you'd apply elsewhere; the "industrial always wins" mindset is dated.

## Sub-type identification

- **Bulk distribution / Class A logistics** — large-format (300K+ SF), modern clear height (32-40+'), institutional-quality
- **Light industrial / shallow bay** — smaller (50-200K SF), often multi-tenant, shorter dock counts
- **Last-mile / infill** — small (often <100K SF) in dense markets near population centers, premium pricing
- **Flex / R&D** — office / industrial blend
- **Cold storage** — specialized; very different operating profile and tenant base
- **Manufacturing** — heavy infrastructure, often single-tenant, often credit-driven

## Physical specs that drive value

- **Clear height** — 32' is institutional minimum; 36' standard for new bulk; 40' for high-cube
- **Dock doors per 10K SF** — typical 1 per 7-10K SF
- **Drive-in doors / loading**
- **Truck court depth** — 130'+ is institutional; <120' is functional limitation
- **Trailer parking ratio** — 1:1 trailer-to-dock or better is institutional
- **Car parking ratio** — 1.5-2 spaces per 1K SF for distribution; higher for flex/manufacturing
- **Column spacing** — 50'×50' minimum for modern; tighter is functional limitation
- **Power capacity** — meaningful for any modern user; especially data center / EV / cold storage tenants
- **Sprinkler / FM Global rating** — ESFR / wet pipe / dry pipe; impacts insurance and tenant requirements

If the OM glosses over physical specs, ask for a building specs sheet.

## Tenant rent roll forensics

For each tenant:
- Square footage occupied, share of total NRA
- Lease structure — typically NNN with full pass-through; some "industrial gross" or modified gross deals
- Base rent (per SF), escalations (3% annual is common; 4% in tighter markets; CPI-linked occasionally)
- Lease term remaining and options
- Tenant credit — public, private with disclosed financials, or undisclosed
- Industry — e-commerce, 3PL, traditional distribution, manufacturing, food, pharma
- Use case — corporate distribution, contract logistics, warehouse-to-warehouse transfer
- Historical performance — recent expansion, downsizing, or stable
- Concessions — TI ($/SF — typically light, $3-15/SF), free rent (1-3 months on a 7-10 year lease)

## Tenant credit and concentration

- Single-tenant or top-3 concentration risk
- Credit rating mix — investment grade vs sub-IG vs unrated
- E-commerce / 3PL tenant exposure (post-COVID demand normalization has hurt these)
- Industries with structural demand drivers (pharma, healthcare, food/grocery) vs those facing headwinds (apparel, electronics)

## Mark-to-market opportunity

In tight markets, in-place rents may be well below market. Ask:
- What's the in-place rent vs. market rent?
- When do leases roll, and what's the recapture timing?
- For value-add deals predicated on roll-to-market, is the market rent assumption defensible? (Industrial broker market rent reports / CoStar / JLL / CBRE / Cushman benchmark)

In softer markets, in-place rents may be ABOVE market — meaning rollover creates downside. Ask the same questions.

## Supply forecasting

The biggest industrial mistake of 2024-2026 has been understating supply.
- Submarket vacancy current and trend (rising in many markets through 2025-2026)
- Under-construction pipeline within submarket and within 30-mile radius
- Speculative vs preleased construction
- New deliveries past 12 months and lease-up velocity
- Asking rents on recent deliveries vs. subject's in-place

Demand the third-party submarket report. CoStar / CBRE / JLL all publish quarterly submarket data. If the OM cites figures, verify.

## Operating expenses

Industrial operating expenses are typically 15-25% of EGI (lower than office or retail because most are pass-through).

- Real estate taxes (large, pass-through)
- Insurance (rising, pass-through)
- Common area maintenance (parking lot, exterior, sprinkler) — modest
- Property management — 1-2% of EGI typical
- Roof reserve — flat-roof industrial has finite life; reserve $0.10-0.30/SF/year
- Sprinkler system maintenance and ITM (inspection / testing / maintenance)

## Capital reserves

- Roof — $5-10/SF replacement, 20-25 year life on TPO/EPDM
- Parking / trucking surfaces — $3-8/SF
- Dock equipment — $5-15K per dock for full refurb
- HVAC (office portion + warehouse make-up air)
- Exterior — paint, sealant
- LED lighting upgrades (typically pre-2015 buildings need this)

## ESG / sustainability

Increasingly relevant for institutional capital:
- Solar potential (roof orientation, structural capacity)
- EV charging capacity / requirements
- LEED / BREEAM certification
- Energy efficiency relative to local benchmarks (LL97 in NY, etc.)

## Industrial-specific questions for the broker

- Tenant-by-tenant rent roll with NRA, in-place rent, escalations, lease structure, options, recapture rights, and credit/parent guarantor?
- Building specs sheet — clear height, dock count, drive-ins, truck court depth, trailer / car parking, column spacing, power capacity, sprinkler system?
- Roof age, condition report, and remaining useful life?
- Recent leasing activity in the building (last 24 months)?
- Submarket leasing velocity, vacancy, and pipeline (CoStar / JLL / CBRE export)?
- Any tenant move-out indications, downsizing, or known non-renewals?
- Tenant credit / financial statements for non-IG tenants?
- Tenant industry mix and concentration to e-commerce / 3PL?
- Property tax bill and projected reassessment on transfer?
- Insurance carrier, premium history, and recent claims?
- Tax appeal status?
- Phase I and PCA?
- Land/expansion potential — additional dock doors, trailer parking, expansion footprint?
- For value-add roll-to-market: comp basis and recent comparable lease deals?
