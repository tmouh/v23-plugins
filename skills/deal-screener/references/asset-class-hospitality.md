# Hospitality Forensics

Hospitality is the most operationally intensive CRE asset class. The "real estate" you're buying is actually a going-concern business with daily revenue. Pro formas are uniquely manipulable here. Apply heavy skepticism.

## Sub-type identification

- **Limited service / select service** — Hampton, Holiday Inn Express, Courtyard, Hyatt Place — efficient operations, 60-65% margin
- **Full service / branded** — Hilton, Marriott, Hyatt full-service flags — more F&B and meeting space; 25-35% margin
- **Lifestyle / boutique** — independent or soft brand (Tribute, Curio, Autograph, Tapestry) — variable margins
- **Resort / destination** — destination markets, leisure-driven, often include amenities / golf / spa
- **Extended stay** — Residence Inn, Homewood, Element, TownePlace — long-stay focus
- **Independent** — no flag, depending entirely on operator and reputation

## STR / market data

The market industry standard is Smith Travel Research (STR). Every hospitality OM should include or reference STR data.

Key metrics:
- **ADR** (Average Daily Rate) — average rate per occupied room
- **Occupancy** — % of available rooms sold
- **RevPAR** = ADR × Occupancy — the unified revenue-per-available-room metric
- **RevPAR Index** = subject RevPAR ÷ comp set RevPAR — should be ≥100 for a stabilized operating asset; ≥110 for outperformers
- **Penetration indices** — separate ADR Index and Occupancy Index reveal whether the property out- or under-performs on rate vs. occupancy

If the OM doesn't include the STR report (or at least the past 36 months of comp set data), demand it. Without STR, you cannot validate the revenue forecast.

## Comp set integrity

The STR comp set is selected by the operator. Sponsors sometimes:
- Include weak hotels in the comp set to inflate the subject's RevPAR Index
- Exclude newer / nicer competitors that compete in reality but make the subject look weak
- Apply a comp set from a different submarket / chain scale

Always ask: "What's in the comp set? Is it the right comp set?" Compare with brand-recommended comp sets and your own knowledge of the market.

## Revenue line items

- **Rooms revenue** — primary; ADR × Occupancy × Available Room Nights
- **F&B revenue** — material for full-service; tied to occupancy and meeting business
- **Meeting / banquet** — separate line; cyclical
- **Other revenue** — parking, spa, retail, resort fees, gift shop, telephone (declining), miscellaneous
- **Total revenue per available room (TRevPAR)** — fuller picture than RevPAR

## Operating margin (House Profit margin)

- Limited service: 60-65% house profit margin
- Select service: 50-60%
- Full service: 25-35%
- Resort: 20-35% (higher operational complexity)

The OM's projected stabilized margin should be benchmarkable. If a full-service hotel projects 45% margin, ask why.

## Cost forensics

- **Rooms department** — labor (housekeeping, front desk, reservations), supplies, commissions, brand fees
- **F&B** — food cost (28-35% of F&B revenue), labor, beverage, etc.
- **Other operated departments** — spa, golf, etc.
- **Undistributed expenses** — admin & general, sales & marketing, repairs & maintenance, utilities, IT
- **Fixed charges** — property tax, insurance, ground rent if applicable
- **Management fees** — base (2-4% of revenue) + incentive (typically 10-15% of GOP above a hurdle)
- **Brand / franchise fees** — royalty (4-6% of rooms revenue), program / marketing (2-4%), reservations (1-3%) — total brand fees commonly 8-13% of rooms revenue
- **FF&E reserve** — required by brand and lender, typically 4-5% of total revenue (ramping up over years 1-4 for new builds)

## PIP cost (critical)

Property Improvement Plans (PIPs) are required by brand on transfer or franchise renewal. PIPs are often the deal-killer that's underwritten too lightly.

- Has the brand provided a PIP for the new owner?
- What's the PIP cost? Compare to OM-modeled capex.
- When is the PIP required to be completed (typically 18-36 months)?
- What's the disruption cost during PIP execution (lost room nights)?
- If the OM doesn't reference a PIP, ask the brand directly or ask the broker for the PIP letter

A $5M underestimated PIP is a 200-300bps drag on going-in yield on a $50M hotel. Material.

## Brand / management contract

- Franchise vs management agreement (huge difference — franchise = owner controls operations; management = operator controls)
- Term remaining
- Termination provisions and fees (some management agreements have brutal termination fees, often >$5-10M)
- Liquidated damages on franchise breach (often 24-36 months of fees as LD)
- Performance test (RevPAR Index threshold) and consequences of failure
- Subordination, non-disturbance, and attornment (SNDA) status with the brand
- Recent operator's track record on this and similar properties

## Capital reserves and capex

- FF&E reserve historical balance vs prescribed (4% of revenue typically)
- Major renovation cycle — typically every 8-12 years for soft goods, every 12-15 for case goods, every 15-20 for major systems
- Recent renovation history and forward 5-year plan

## Demand drivers

- **Group vs transient mix** — group is more contractual; transient is more rate-volatile
- **Corporate vs leisure mix** — corporate softens in recessions; leisure has been bull-whip-volatile post-COVID
- **Booking pace** — forward 30/60/90/180-day pace vs. same time prior year
- **Channel mix** — direct, brand.com, OTA (Expedia, Booking), GDS, group — OTA dependence reduces ADR
- **Demand drivers in the market** — convention center calendar, corporate HQ presence, leisure seasonality, airport traffic

## Hospitality-specific questions for the broker

- Full STR report (comp set + 36 months historical + brand reports if available)?
- Brand/franchisor's PIP letter for the new owner — and PIP cost estimate?
- Current management or franchise agreement (full document)?
- Operator's historical performance — RevPAR Index trend by month for trailing 24 months?
- Booking pace report (forward 30/60/90/180 days)?
- Group calendar (commitments through next 24 months) and recent pipeline?
- Channel mix breakdown?
- F&B revenue detail and recent trends?
- FF&E reserve current balance?
- Capex history (last 5 years) and forward plan (next 5 years)?
- Property tax — current bill, reassessment projection?
- Insurance — including business interruption and named windstorm if applicable?
- Phase I and PCA?
- For seasonal markets: monthly revenue and occupancy detail for trailing 36 months?
- Major group bookings or losses in the recent past?
- For resort: golf rounds, spa revenue, F&B by outlet, retail revenue?
- Labor situation — unionized? (UNITE HERE jurisdictions especially), recent contract status, key personnel turnover?
