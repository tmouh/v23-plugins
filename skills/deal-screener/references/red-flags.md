# Master Red-Flag Library

The universal red flags that apply across asset classes. Asset-class-specific red flags live in the per-class reference files. The forensic mindset section in SKILL.md gives the philosophy; this file gives the checklist.

A senior reviewer reads with this list as a mental checklist. Not every flag will fire on every deal. But you should be able to say, for each flag below, "I checked and this isn't an issue here" — not just glide past.

## A. Pricing & basis

- **Markup over seller's basis.** What did the seller pay, when, what did they invest? Markups >50% over the prior trade with no transformative capex are suspicious. Look for the prior sale on public record. If unavailable, ask.
- **Comp cherry-picking.** Are the cited "comp trades" all the highest-priced? Is the OM filtering by "Class A only" when the subject is Class B? Are comps from tighter submarkets being applied here? Pull at least one comp the OM didn't include.
- **Per-unit / per-SF basis vs. replacement cost.** If the basis is at or above replacement cost, the value-add story has to be bulletproof. Flag and stress-test.
- **Off-market premium.** "Off-market" pricing is sometimes a euphemism for "we couldn't clear at this price in a broad process." Ask whether this was truly never marketed.
- **Implied yield vs. cost of debt.** Going-in cap rate at or below current senior debt cost = negative leverage. Ask the sponsor to defend going-in negative leverage with a specific value-creation thesis.

## B. NOI quality

- **T-12 vs T-3 divergence.** Always compare. If T-3 annualized > T-12 by >10%, the recent trajectory is being annualized aggressively. If T-3 < T-12, something is deteriorating.
- **NOI definition shifts.** OM may use "Cash NOI," "Adjusted NOI," "Stabilized Pro Forma NOI" — each different. The going-in cap rate should be Year 1 contractual NOI on the ACTUAL trailing financials, not a forward number. Flag any NOI presentation that excludes recurring expenses (replacement reserves, management fees) without disclosure.
- **Other income inflation.** RUBS, pet rent, parking, late fees, tech fees, package fees — easy to model as "upside" but rarely fully realizable. Compare other income as % of GPR to comparable assets.
- **Concession run-off treatment.** If the property is in lease-up or recently leased, current NOI may benefit from concessions burning off. Confirm the burn-off math.
- **Bad debt / collection loss.** Often modeled at 0.5-1% when 2-3% is more honest, especially for B/C class. Compare to T-12 actual collection loss.
- **Loss-to-lease as "upside."** L2L is the gap between in-place rent and stated market rent. Sponsors often present 100% capture as automatic. Real capture is dictated by lease expiration timing, tenant retention, and whether stated market rents are actually achievable.

## C. Operating expenses

- **Tax bump-up.** Most jurisdictions reassess on sale. The new tax bill at the new basis should be modeled. If OM uses seller's tax bill projected forward, that's a material understatement. Compute the tax bump-up yourself using the local mill rate / assessment ratio. Always.
- **Insurance.** Especially in coastal FL, TX, CA, LA — insurance has 2-3x'd in many submarkets since 2020. If the OM uses the seller's insurance, ask for current renewal quotes. The number is often 50-100bps of cap rate.
- **Management fee market check.** 2-4% of EGI for multifamily, 2-3% for office/retail, 1-2% for industrial. If absent or below market, often hidden in a related-party arrangement.
- **R&M / capex blending.** OM may classify what should be capex (roof, HVAC) as R&M to inflate cap-rate-supporting NOI. Look for a separate replacement reserve line; it should exist.
- **Payroll.** If the sponsor plans to "operate more efficiently" with lower payroll, ask how — specifically what positions get cut.
- **Utility cost.** Especially in markets with deregulated power (TX, parts of NE) — utilities can swing dramatically. Check if reimbursed (RUBS) and at what capture rate.

## D. Pro forma assumptions

- **Rent growth above market trend.** Compare projected rent growth (often 3-4%) to historical trailing 5-year market growth. If projected exceeds historical, ask why.
- **Vacancy assumption below market.** Should be at or above market for stabilized year. Sub-market assumptions are aggressive.
- **Cap-rate compression.** Exit cap < entry cap = sponsor expects yield compression over hold. Ask why specifically. Default skeptical.
- **Refinance assumption.** When does the sponsor refi? At what rate? At what DSCR / debt yield? Stress-test refi at +100bps and see if refi is even feasible.
- **Capex schedule.** Is the value-add capex front-loaded (good — actually executes) or back-end-loaded (hides equity needs)? Is contingency built in?
- **Lease-up pace.** For value-add or new construction, what's the assumed lease-up velocity? Compare to comparable lease-ups in the submarket.

## E. Capital stack & sponsor economics

- **Total fee load.** Acquisition + AM + financing + disposition + construction mgmt + refi fees. Sum as % of LP equity over hold. If GP is making 5%+ of LP equity in fees regardless of performance, alignment is weak.
- **Promote structure.** When does the GP start participating? What's the catch-up? Is there a lookback / true-up at exit? IRR-based vs equity multiple-based promotes can produce dramatically different outcomes.
- **GP co-invest.** Real money, or paper alignment? "5% co-invest" funded by deferred fees is not skin in the game.
- **Pref clip.** What's the pref rate, is it accruing or current-pay, does it compound? Crucial for downside scenarios.
- **Lender protections.** Recourse vs non-recourse, completion guarantees, bad-boy carve-outs, cash management triggers.
- **Mezz / pref equity terms.** Often the most expensive piece of the stack. PIK vs cash-pay. Acceleration triggers.

## F. Sponsor & alignment

- **Track record beyond this deal.** Is the OM showing one prior deal as the entire track record? How many full-cycle deals?
- **Asset class / market / strategy fit.** Has this sponsor done THIS specific thing before? A multifamily sponsor doing their first office deal is meaningfully different.
- **Related-party transactions.** Manager, GC, broker, lender — any owned by sponsor or affiliates? Disclosed? Priced at market?
- **Recent fund performance.** If sponsor has prior funds, what's recent performance? Are they stuck on illiquid positions?
- **Litigation, fund freezes, redemption gates.** Always ask. Sometimes disclosed in the IC packet, often not.
- **Departures.** Has a key principal left recently? Especially the one whose track record is being credited?

## G. Property physical

- **Age and major capex events.** Roof, mechanicals, parking lot, façade — when was each last addressed? When does each need to be addressed during the hold?
- **Phase I / environmental.** Existence and conclusions. Any RECs (Recognized Environmental Conditions)?
- **Property Condition Assessment.** Existence, age, deferred maintenance summary.
- **Flood / windstorm / earthquake zone.** Often material in FL, TX, LA, CA. Insurance implication.
- **Zoning / entitlements.** Especially for value-add with density addition or change of use. Are entitlements in hand or assumed?
- **Title / survey.** Liens, easements, encroachments, access issues.
- **Ground lease.** If applicable — terms, remaining years, escalations, mortgageability.

## H. Market

- **Supply pipeline understated.** Comps reports (CoStar, Yardi Matrix, AxioMetrics, REIS) often show planned and under-construction projects the OM omits. Always ask for the supply pipeline.
- **Demand drivers asymmetric.** "Job growth" and "population growth" are often national averages applied locally. Drill into the actual MSA or submarket.
- **Submarket vs metro confusion.** OM may cite metro fundamentals (e.g., DFW unemployment) when submarket fundamentals (e.g., the specific zip code) are materially different.
- **Tenant demand softness.** For office/retail/industrial — sublease space, leasing velocity, deal counts. Often hidden.

## I. Structural / process flags

- **The "best and final" pressure.** "We're calling for offers Friday" is sometimes real, sometimes manufactured. Don't let timeline manufacture rush your screen.
- **Tight diligence window.** A 3-week DD window on a complex value-add deal is a tell. Ask for more time; if denied, ask why.
- **No model.** OM presents IRR/multiple but won't share the model. Ask for it. If denied, the screen is necessarily directional.
- **Limited audience.** "Off-market, only shown to a few" — sometimes legitimate, sometimes the deal failed in a wider process. Ask.
- **Recent prior failed sale.** Has this property been on the market before in the last 2-3 years? If so, what happened? Why is it back?

## J. Disclosure quality flags

- **Aggregated rent roll.** OM shows "average rent" instead of unit-by-unit. Always ask for the full rent roll.
- **Aggregated tenant data.** Office/retail/industrial — "blended WALT" without per-tenant detail. Always ask for tenant-by-tenant breakout including credit ratings, lease structure, escalations, options, recapture.
- **Footnotes that move material numbers.** Numbers that look great until you read footnote 14, which says "assumes refinance at 5.0% in Year 3" or "excludes capex of $X."
- **Currency and units.** $/SF vs $/unit vs $/key vs $/pad — make sure cited metrics use the right denominator for the asset class.
