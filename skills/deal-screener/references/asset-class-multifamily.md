# Multifamily Forensics

This file covers what to look at when the asset is multifamily (conventional apartments, BTR, student, senior, MHC) — beyond the universal items in `red-flags.md`. Read alongside, not instead of.

## Rent roll forensics

The rent roll is the foundation. Always ask for the full unit-by-unit roll if not in the OM. From it, recompute:

- **Average in-place rent per unit type** (1BR, 2BR, etc.) — compare to the OM's stated "in-place rent"
- **Loss-to-lease (L2L)** = (stated market rent − in-place rent) / in-place rent. The OM often presents L2L as automatic upside; real capture is gated by lease expiration timing and tenant retention.
- **Lease expiration distribution** — how many leases roll in the first 12 months? Concentration of expirations creates risk on retention, opportunity on lift.
- **Rent variance within unit type** — same floorplans should have similar rents within ~5%. Wide variance suggests inconsistent leasing standards or model unit included.
- **Concession run-off** — how many of the in-place leases are within their first 12 months and signed with concession packages? Their burn-off contributes to NOI growth that should be modeled honestly.
- **Down units / non-revenue units** — model units, employee units, vacant units in renovation. Do they show as $0 rent or as a placeholder?

## Value-add lift verification

If the deal is value-add, the central question is: **is the projected post-renovation rent achievable?**

- **What's the comparable basis?** Demand a list of competing properties used as the rent benchmark. Verify those properties (a) actually exist, (b) are truly comparable in vintage / location / amenity / unit mix, (c) are not in a tighter submarket.
- **Renovation premium track record.** Sponsor should be able to point to executed renovations on similar product where the projected lift was achieved. Ask for the case study.
- **Renovation cost.** Per-unit renovation cost should be benchmarkable. $5K/unit is light; $25K/unit is heavy (in-unit upgrades + amenities + exterior). Compare to GC bids if available.
- **Pace.** How many units per month can be turned without triggering vacancy spikes? Most operators do 10-20 units/month at a 200-unit asset. Sponsor projections of 30+/month are aggressive.
- **Down-time per unit.** Each renovation creates 2-4 weeks of vacancy on that unit. Is this in the model?
- **Classic vs Renovated rent comp.** Often the OM compares to renovated units in the comp set — but the subject's unrenovated units are what's currently leased. The classic-vs-classic spread is what matters.

## Operating expense forensics for MF

- **Tax bump-up.** Multifamily is reassessed in most states on transfer. Use local mill rate × new assessed value (often = 80-100% of purchase price depending on state). Compare to OM. Texas, Florida, Arizona are particularly aggressive with reassessment.
- **Property insurance.** Large drivers in coastal and CAT-prone markets. Ask for actual renewal quotes. $0.50/SF is light; $1.50/SF is meaningful in Florida.
- **Payroll** — typical for 200-300 unit asset is 1 community manager, 1 leasing, 1 maintenance lead, 1 maintenance tech (4 staff), at $200-300K all-in including benefits.
- **R&M, turnover, and capex.** Turnover cost per turn is typically $1,500-3,500 depending on level of unit refresh. Annual R&M ex-turn is typically $400-600/unit/year. Replacement reserves should be ≥$300/unit/year for older product.
- **Utilities.** Even with full RUBS reimbursement, capture rate is typically 80-90%, not 100%. Bad debt eats some, vacant units eat the rest.
- **Marketing.** Leasing fees, ILS subscriptions (Apartments.com, Zillow Rentals), referral programs. $300-500/move-in is typical; can spike higher in lease-up.

## Other income honesty test

OM often shows growing "Other Income." Check what's in it:
- Pet rent / pet fees (capture by % of pet-eligible residents — typically 30-40%)
- Parking ($25-100/space/month for surface; $100-300 for covered/garage; capture ≤80%)
- Storage (limited; $30-60/month per unit)
- Late fees (often $25-75 — meaningful in B/C class but limited capture)
- Application fees (one-time; spread across move-ins ≠ recurring revenue)
- Tech / package fees (becoming standard $25-50/unit/month — 95% capture)
- RUBS / utility reimbursements (separate category, see above)

If Other Income > 8-10% of GPR, dig in. It's often inflated or has one-time items lumped in.

## Concessions

Concessions are the most-buried line in MF screens. Watch for:
- "Net effective rent" vs. "stated rent" — net effective accounts for concessions amortized; stated does not
- Lease term ratio (12-month vs 13-month vs 15-month leases at same headline rent — longer terms with one month free are very different from 12-month leases at headline)
- Concession trend (increasing concessions in a market = softening; OMs often show static)

## Vacancy / collection loss

- Stabilized economic vacancy assumption should be ≥7% for typical product, ≥9% for student / lease-up properties
- Collection loss / bad debt should be ≥1% for A class, ≥2% for B/C class — many OMs use 0.5%
- Sum of (physical vacancy + concessions + bad debt + L2L not captured) is the real economic vacancy. Often quietly 12-18%, not the 5-7% headline.

## Market & supply

- Submarket vacancy from CoStar / AxioMetrics / Yardi Matrix — ask for the report
- Permitting and under-construction supply within 3-mile radius — typically the OM understates
- Class A delivering nearby will impact Class B/C through trickle-down concessions
- Job growth at MSA level — but apply at submarket where possible
- Rent growth — historical and projected; compare OM projection to submarket trend

## Multifamily-specific questions for the broker

- Full unit-by-unit rent roll (not aggregated)?
- T-3, T-12, and prior-year financials in detail (P&L by line)?
- Renovation case studies on prior projects with achieved rent vs. projected?
- Renovation cost detail (GC quotes or sponsor budget breakdown)?
- Insurance renewal quotes (current carriers, current premium)?
- Property tax assessor's projected reassessment for new owner?
- Phase I and PCA?
- Submarket supply pipeline (CoStar / Axio export)?
- Concession history by month for trailing 18 months?
- Bad debt / collection loss actual by month for trailing 18 months?
- Resident turnover / retention rate?
- Eviction filings and outcomes for trailing 12 months?
- Any pending or threatened resident litigation (esp. habitability claims)?
- Section 8 / voucher penetration (if any)?
- Rent control / stabilization status (in CA, NY, NJ, OR, MN, etc.)?
