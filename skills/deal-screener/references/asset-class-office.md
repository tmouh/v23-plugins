# Office Forensics

Office is the hardest asset class to screen in 2026. The fundamentals are still volatile post-pandemic, supply is in many markets effectively obsolete, and capital availability is tight. Apply heightened skepticism to any office OM.

## The threshold question

Before going line-by-line, answer: **Why does this asset have demand in 5 years?** Specifically:
- Is this trophy / Class A+ in a tier-1 CBD with quality flight tailwinds? (Possibly defensible.)
- Is this a B/B+ in a flight-quality-loser submarket? (Almost certainly impaired — heavy skepticism.)
- Is this a conversion candidate (to residential, lab, mixed-use, hospitality)? (Underwrite the conversion path, not the office hold.)
- Is this a single-tenant or near-single-tenant with credit lease? (Underwrite as a credit bond, not office real estate.)
- Is this medical office (MOB) or lab? (Different asset class — see references/asset-class-other.md.)

If the OM treats a B-class office in a struggling submarket as a routine "value-add lease-up," that itself is a red flag.

## Rent roll forensics for office

Demand a tenant-by-tenant rent roll. From it, recompute:

- **WALT** (weighted average lease term) by tenant — by NRA-weighted and revenue-weighted
- **Rollover by year** — concentration of expirations is rollover risk
- **In-place rent vs market rent** by tenant / floor
- **Concession structure** — TI ($/SF), free rent (months), commissions baked in
- **Lease structure** — full-service / modified gross / NNN / industrial gross. Each shifts who pays opex. Misclassification by the OM is common.
- **Escalations** — fixed bumps (2-3%/year), CPI-linked, or tied to opex pass-through
- **Options** — extension options at fair market vs. predetermined rate; expansion / contraction options; termination options (huge risk if tenant has unilateral termination at fair-market knockout)

## Tenant credit

- Are tenants public companies, private with disclosed financials, or undisclosed?
- For credit tenants, current rating (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) and recent rating action
- For private tenants, financials, parent guarantee status, and industry exposure
- Concentration risk: largest tenant by NRA, by NOI; top 5 tenants
- Industry concentration: tech-heavy roll, finance-heavy roll, government, etc. — concentration risk if industry softens

## TI / LC / capital reserves

This is where office deals get sneaky.

- **Tenant Improvements** — typical CBD A class: $80-150/SF for new leases, $30-60/SF for renewals. Suburban: $50-100/SF new, $20-40/SF renewal. Lower numbers in OM = under-reserved.
- **Leasing commissions** — typically 5-6% of gross lease value for new, 2-3% for renewal
- **Free rent** — typically 6-12 months on a 7-10 year lease in current market; OMs sometimes ignore in cash flow
- **Capital reserves** — building systems, lobby refresh, common area, parking — $1-3/SF/year typical for B+ stabilized

If the OM under-reserves TI/LC, that's an NOI capitalization that's hiding required capex. Recompute on a stabilized basis using market TI/LC; compare to OM.

## Sublease space

In 2026, sublease availability is still elevated in many CBDs. The sublease market sets the floor for direct asking rents.

- Submarket sublease availability (typically 15-30% of total available in soft CBDs)
- Sublease asking rents (typically 20-40% below direct)
- Whether the subject's tenants have placed space on the sublet market (a current tenant subletting is a lease-renewal flight risk)
- Whether the OM acknowledges or minimizes sublease impact

## Utilization data

Increasingly available via badge data, cell-phone foot traffic, or operator reporting:
- Daily utilization rate (% of leased SF actually occupied on a typical Tuesday)
- 4 day vs 5 day utilization patterns (most CBDs are 4-day now)
- Trend: rising, flat, or falling
- If sponsor has utilization data, ask for it. If they don't, that's also information.

## Operating expenses

- Real estate taxes — material; reassessment on transfer common
- Utilities — significant (HVAC for vacant floors still costs money)
- Cleaning / janitorial — typically $1-2/SF
- Security — varies widely, $1-3/SF in CBDs
- Property management — 2-3% of EGI typical
- Common area maintenance — $1-2/SF base, more for amenitized buildings
- Insurance — going up significantly
- Compare on $/SF basis to BOMA / industry benchmarks for the market

## Conversion / repositioning thesis (if applicable)

If the deal is pitched as a conversion play (office to multifamily / hotel / lab):
- Is the building physically suitable? Floor plate depth (60-65 ft ideal for resi conversion), window line, column spacing, mechanical capacity, plumbing risers
- Zoning — by-right for the new use, or requires entitlement?
- Conversion cost per SF — $200-400/SF for resi conversion is typical in 2026
- Conversion timeline — 18-36 months minimum
- Permitting and entitlement risk (especially in NY, SF, etc.)
- Does the deal pencil at conversion-ready cost (current basis + conversion capex), with an exit at the converted use's market values?
- If conversion is the actual thesis, the OM should be priced to a conversion-ready basis, not stabilized office NOI

## Office-specific questions for the broker

- Tenant-by-tenant rent roll with NRA, in-place rent, lease structure, expiration, escalations, options, TI/LC remaining, and credit / parent guarantor?
- Recent leasing activity (last 24 months) — new leases and renewals with terms?
- Subleased SF on the building, plus broader submarket sublease data?
- Building utilization data (badge / sensor / operator estimate)?
- Capital improvements completed in last 5 years and planned?
- Building specs — floor plate, slab-to-slab, mechanical, life safety, ADA?
- Phase I, PCA, and any structural / envelope reports?
- Tax bill, pending appeal status, projected reassessment?
- Insurance carrier, premium history, recent claims?
- Sponsor / current owner's leasing strategy and any recent broker change?
- For conversion thesis: feasibility study, zoning analysis, and entitlement status?
- Submarket leasing velocity, vacancy, and pipeline (third-party report)?
- Tenant move-out indications or known non-renewals?

## When to recommend pass quickly

For office deals, a fast pass is often the right answer. Recommend pass when:
- Class B/C in a soft submarket without a clear conversion path
- Rollover concentrated in a short window with no clear renewal indicator
- Major tenant publicly downsizing footprint
- Replacement cost is dramatically below offered price (i.e., basis above replacement)
- Sponsor has no track record in office or in this submarket
- Conversion thesis without a feasibility study

A clean fast pass is more valuable than a 12-page screen on a deal that's a no.
