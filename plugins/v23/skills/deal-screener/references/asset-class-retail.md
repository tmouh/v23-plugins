# Retail Forensics

Retail breaks into very different sub-types: grocery-anchored neighborhood, power center, lifestyle, mall, single-tenant net lease (STNL), urban street retail, and outlet. The lens varies. Identify the sub-type before applying detail.

## Sub-type identification

- **Grocery-anchored neighborhood** — anchored by a top-tier grocer (Publix, Kroger, HEB, Wegmans, Trader Joe's, Whole Foods) — generally the most defensible retail format
- **Power center** — big-box anchored, typically junior anchors and inline shop space
- **Lifestyle / mixed-use** — open-air, often F&B / experiential / fitness mix
- **Mall** — enclosed, dying as a category except for top-tier "A++ mall" properties
- **Strip / unanchored** — small inline strips with no anchor
- **STNL** — single tenant on long-term net lease (more credit-driven than real-estate-driven)
- **Urban street retail** — high-street, gateway market, very different liquidity dynamics
- **Power-of-X / specialty** — outlet, entertainment-anchored, etc.

## Anchor / inline structure

For anchored retail (most categories):
- Who is the anchor, what's the lease term, what's the rent structure?
- Is the anchor on a flat fixed rent or % of sales?
- Is there a co-tenancy clause? (If anchor leaves, do inlines have rent reduction or termination right?)
- Is the anchor's lease nearly expired? (If <5 years remaining at acquisition, it's effectively a value-add bet on retention.)
- What's the anchor's financial health and recent same-store sales trend?
- Does the OM disclose the anchor's sales data? (Most anchored leases have sales reporting requirements; ask if not provided.)

For grocery anchors specifically:
- Sales/SF — top-tier grocers do $700-900+/SF, weak operators do $350-500/SF
- Trade area trends — population growth, daytime population, household income
- Store rank within the chain (top-quartile stores are stable; bottom-quartile face closure risk)
- Recent capital invested (remodel age, equipment age)

## Inline tenant analysis

For each inline tenant:
- Use category (food, services, fitness, fashion, health, etc.)
- Lease term, rent (base + percentage rent), escalations
- Termination / kickout rights
- Co-tenancy provisions (especially anchor co-tenancy)
- Sales (if disclosed); occupancy cost as % of sales
- Recent or pending bankruptcy (chains: see Bed Bath, Tuesday Morning, Christmas Tree Shops, etc.)

## Occupancy cost

The single most important inline tenant metric. Occupancy cost = (rent + CAM + tax recovery + insurance recovery + percentage rent) ÷ tenant sales.

- Healthy occupancy cost typically 8-15% depending on category (food service tolerates 10-12%; soft goods 6-10%; jewelry / specialty up to 15%)
- Occupancy cost above category benchmark = tenant likely struggling, renewal risk
- Occupancy cost well below benchmark = tenant has lots of room (or is in a captive lease)

## E-commerce risk

For each inline:
- Category exposure to e-commerce (apparel, electronics, books = high; food service, services, experiential = low)
- Recent same-store sales trend if disclosed
- Whether the tenant has been closing stores nationally

## Lease structure honesty

- **NNN vs absolute net** — NNN passes through CAM/tax/insurance; absolute net passes through everything including roof and structure. Different operating risk.
- **CAM caps** — limits on how much CAM can grow (typically a non-controllable / controllable split; controllable capped at 5%/year)
- **Recovery exclusions** — tenants often exclude management fee, capital items, etc. Reduces effective recovery rate.
- **Recovery rate** — actual recovered $ / total recoverable expenses. Often 75-90%, not 100%.

## Tax and insurance pass-through

- Are tenants on full tax / insurance pass-through, or stops / base year?
- If stops, the landlord eats inflation above the stop year
- If insurance has spiked (FL/TX) and tenants are on stop, the landlord absorbs a meaningful expense increase

## Capex reserves

- Roof life (typically 20-25 years for newer TPO; flag if approaching end of life)
- Parking lot resurfacing ($3-8/SF roughly every 15-20 years)
- HVAC (rooftop units, $5-10K/unit, 15-20 year life)
- Anchor TI for renewal (anchors often demand $20-50/SF TI to renew)
- Inline TI for new leases ($30-80/SF) and renewals ($10-30/SF)

## Market & competitive dynamics

- Trade area demographics — population, income, daytime population, traffic counts
- Competing centers within 3-5 miles, anchored by what
- Recent or planned competitive openings / closings
- Vacancy rate in the trade area for similar product

## Retail-specific questions for the broker

- Tenant-by-tenant rent roll including base rent, CAM/tax/insurance recovery structure, percentage rent provisions, lease term, options, termination/kickout rights, co-tenancy, and security deposits?
- Tenant sales data for tenants under sales reporting (especially anchor)?
- Anchor lease — full document, including any termination provisions, options, and recent amendments?
- Recent leasing activity (last 24 months) and the renewal velocity / retention rate?
- Vacancy and lease-up history for trailing 3 years?
- CAM reconciliation for trailing 2 years showing recovery rate by tenant?
- Property condition assessment (roof age, parking, HVAC, asphalt)?
- Co-tenancy events triggered or threatened in the last 2 years?
- Trade area demographics report?
- Competing supply within 5 miles — recent openings and planned developments?
- For STNL: full lease, parent guaranty, tenant financial statements, store-level financials if obtainable?
