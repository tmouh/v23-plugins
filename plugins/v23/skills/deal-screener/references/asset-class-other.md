# Other Asset Classes

This file covers asset classes that don't fit cleanly into multifamily / office / retail / industrial / hospitality. Each section is briefer than the dedicated reference files; flag this to the user and dig deeper if a deal in one of these classes lands.

## Self-storage

- **Sub-types** — drive-up vs. climate-controlled vs. wine storage; primary vs secondary market; urban vs suburban
- **Operator** — REIT-flagged (Public Storage, Extra Space, CubeSmart, Life Storage) vs. small operator vs. self-managed
- **Occupancy economics** — physical occupancy and economic occupancy; concessions (often "first month free," sometimes "rent locked for 6 months")
- **Rental rate management** — algorithmic ECRI (existing customer rate increases) is the primary revenue lever; new-customer street rates set the supply curve
- **Supply** — submarket new supply has spiked in many markets 2020-2024; check 3-mile-radius pipeline. Self-storage is one of the most permeable supply markets — easy to overbuild.
- **Demand drivers** — population mobility, household formation, downsizing, divorce, e-commerce returns
- **Operating margin** — 65-75% NOI margin at stabilization is industry standard; below that suggests inefficiency
- **Specific questions:** Submarket supply pipeline (Radius+, BBX, Inside Self-Storage)? Recent rate moves on subject? Promotional concessions used? Auction frequency and write-offs? Climate-controlled mix? On-site manager structure?

## Mobile Home Communities (MHC) / Manufactured Housing

- **Park type** — all-age vs 55+; tenant-owned-home vs park-owned-home (POH) mix
- **Lot rent** — typical $400-700/month base, with utility reimbursements
- **Owner-occupancy ratio** — % of homes owned by residents; healthier when high
- **Capital structure** — pad value vs. POH inventory value (POH is asset-class hybrid)
- **Operating margin** — typically 60-70% NOI margin; very lean operating model
- **Capex needs** — water/sewer (especially if private utilities), roads, common areas, lots
- **Regulatory** — rent control / rent stabilization in CA and parts of FL; HUD code requirements
- **Specific questions:** Lot rent history (last 5 years)? POH count and unit-by-unit financials? Resident demographics (income / job stability)? Regulatory exposure (utilities, rent control)? Park-vs-tenant ownership of utility infrastructure? Capex deferred maintenance?

## Data Center

This is heavily specialized; treat as a separate asset class entirely.

- **Type** — hyperscale vs. retail colo vs. wholesale
- **Power capacity (MW)** — the primary value driver, more than physical SF
- **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)** — efficiency metric (lower is better; 1.2-1.4 is institutional)
- **Tenant credit and lease structure** — generally long-term (10-15 years), rate-fixed, often with annual escalations and CPI components
- **Power source / sustainability** — increasingly material for ESG-mandated capital
- **Connectivity / carrier neutrality** — number of fiber providers, peering ecosystem
- **Cooling system** — air, liquid, immersion; relates to PUE and density
- **Power utility availability** — most US markets have multi-year power waitlists by 2025-2026; existing power-secured assets have premium value
- **Specific questions:** Power contracts (term, capacity, escalation, source)? Tenant lease structure with power passthrough? Carrier connectivity? PUE? Future expansion capacity for power and floor space? Sustainability profile?

## Mixed-Use

- **Identify the dominant component by NOI** — that's the primary asset class lens; treat others as secondary
- **Cross-component dependencies** — does retail depend on office foot traffic? Does residential value depend on retail amenitization? These create concentration / correlation risk.
- **Operational complexity** — multiple property managers, brand specialists, lease structures
- **Capital allocation** — different components have different capex cycles
- **Apply each relevant asset-class file to its component**

## Land

- **Entitlement status** — by-right buildable, requires variance, or fully unentitled
- **Zoning** — what's allowed, what's the FAR / density, height, parking minimums
- **Highest-and-best-use analysis** — what use, at what price, supports what residual land value
- **Site characteristics** — utilities to the site, topography, environmental conditions, soils
- **Carrying cost** — taxes, insurance, debt service, security; often material on multi-year holds
- **Take-out** — who's the buyer at exit (a developer, an end user, a builder)
- **Specific questions:** Entitlement timeline if not in hand? Survey, environmental, geotechnical reports? Utility connection costs? Tax bill while undeveloped? Comparable land sales per buildable SF / per unit?

## Medical Office Building (MOB)

- **Tenant base** — hospital-affiliated vs. independent practitioners
- **Hospital-on-campus vs. off-campus** — on-campus carries hospital credit halo and typically lower vacancy
- **Tenant credit** — health system parent guarantees, physician group credit
- **Lease structure** — typically NNN with longer terms (10+ years) than conventional office
- **TI / capex** — medical TI is heavy ($150-300/SF for full medical fit-out vs. $80-150/SF for office)
- **Specialty mix** — single-specialty vs multi-specialty; ambulatory surgery vs. clinic mix
- **Specific questions:** Tenant rent roll with specialty, hospital affiliation, lease structure, term, credit? Hospital relationship if on-campus or affiliated? Recent leasing? Capex history including specialty buildout? Property tax abatement / nonprofit status?

## Lab / Life Sciences

- **Submarket** — Cambridge MA, San Francisco peninsula, San Diego, RTP, etc. — hyper-localized clusters
- **Tenant credit** — biotech tenants often pre-revenue, dependent on funding cycles; check funding status
- **Lab fit-out** — wet lab vs dry lab; typical $200-500/SF buildout cost
- **Conversion candidates** — many former office buildings being converted; check feasibility
- **Recent oversupply** — many markets (especially Cambridge / Boston, SF, San Diego) have soft fundamentals 2025-2026 due to overbuild and biotech funding pullback
- **Specific questions:** Tenant credit (funding status for pre-revenue biotechs), lab specs (HVAC, ventilation, lab gas, vibration tolerance), recent leasing velocity, sublease availability in submarket, conversion feasibility if applicable

## Senior Housing

- **Care levels** — independent living (IL), assisted living (AL), memory care (MC), skilled nursing (SNF), CCRC blends
- **Operator quality** — paramount; senior housing is operationally intensive
- **Acuity mix** — affects revenue, expenses, regulatory environment
- **Length of stay** — IL ~3+ years, AL ~2 years, MC ~18 months, SNF much shorter
- **Reimbursement source mix** — private pay vs. Medicare vs. Medicaid (Medicaid for SNF; very different economics)
- **Regulatory** — state-by-state licensing, especially for AL/MC/SNF
- **Operating margin** — IL 35-45%, AL 25-35%, MC 25-35%, SNF 10-20%
- **Specific questions:** Operator track record and operator agreement structure, current census and trend, recent rate increases and resistance, regulatory citations, staffing ratios and labor cost trend, building physical condition (especially mechanical and life safety)

## Niche / Specialty

If a deal lands in a niche class not covered above (cold storage, parking, EV charging, fuel/c-store, marina, golf, mortuary, agricultural land, billboard, oil/gas mineral, etc.), flag it to the user and ask:
- Does V23 want a screening at all, or a recommendation to bring in a specialist?
- What specifically does V23 know about this asset class, and what should I focus on?

Better to ask than to apply generic CRE underwriting to a specialty class where the rules differ.
