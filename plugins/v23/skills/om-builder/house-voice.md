VANADIUM HOUSE VOICE + ANTI-AI-TELL RULESET
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This file governs HOW WORDS ARE WRITTEN in every Vanadium OM — new-build prose,
revised prose, headlines, KPI labels, captions, source lines. CC applies it when
authoring content (Phase 7); the polish prompt hands it to CIP as a verifier.

Two parts:
  PART 1 — the Vanadium house voice (extracted verbatim from production decks:
           105 N 13th, Obsidian Platform Deck, Ever Leaf, Krios).
  PART 2 — the anti-AI-tell ruleset (what NOT to do; research-backed).

These reinforce each other: the Vanadium voice — specific numbers, named actors,
the bear case stated plainly, varied rhythm — is itself the strongest defense
against sounding machine-written. Write in the voice and most AI tells vanish as
a side effect. The anti-AI ruleset is the backstop.

PART 1 — THE VANADIUM HOUSE VOICE (13 rules, each with a real example)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The register: plain-spoken institutional. Confident, never ornate. It reads like
a sharp principal talking to another principal — not a marketing brochure. Every
rule below is drawn from how the firm actually writes.

**1. Open every section with the conclusion, not a label.** State the claim that
already contains the answer, then prove it.
  Ex: "The simple business plan generates high returns: $35.5 million net profit;
  an 8.2% yield-on-cost, and levered returns >24.00% IRR and >2.00x equity
  multiple on a 5-year hold."

**2. Vary rhythm deliberately — a long, fact-dense sentence, then a short
declarative hammer.** Use fragments for punch.
  Ex: "Capital cannot compress grid physics." / "Buy. Develop. Sell. Earn Through
  the Hold." / "Same Thesis. Different Unlock."

**3. Replace adjectives with numbers, deltas, and comparisons.** If something is
strong, quantify it and benchmark it — don't assert it.
  Ex: "Class A office rents command a 42% premium to Class B" (NOT "rents are
  very strong").

**4. Attach a unit, a YoY/QoQ delta, and a source to every metric.** Returns to
two decimals.
  Ex: "2025 leasing up 17.1% YoY (35.1 MM SF) and Q4 volume up 26.3% QoQ"; footer
  "Source: Colliers, Brooklyn Office Market, Q4 2025."

**5. Name every actor and give its pedigree or scale.** No anonymous "a developer"
or "a major fund."
  Ex: "developed by Simon Dushinsky (Rabsky Group), one of Brooklyn's most
  prolific residential developers"; "Rockpool Investments, a UK-based private
  equity firm with ~£1.7bn AUM."

**6. State the bear case plainly, then neutralize it in the same breath.** Never
hide a weakness — use it as the setup for the opportunity.
  Ex: "The asset is currently stuck searching for a single hyperscale tenant…
  [value-add:] Convert single-tenant BTS into a laddered campus… diversified
  risk." On 105: openly recounts the prior developer's "6 bridge loans and a
  combination of various missteps and prolonged delays," then explains why the
  reset basis makes the same asset work.

**7. Prove the plan with precedent — show the sponsor already did this.** Track
record is the primary evidence, not a footnote.
  Ex: "Sponsor followed this same business plan at 315 Meserole… Within 10 months
  leased >18,000 SF"; "Cloverleaf built a 10–15 GW US pipeline in under two years.
  PEL Infrastructure is the European sequel."

**8. Anchor every claim to a benchmark.** Comparison is the default move (Class A
vs B, US vs EU, comp sales vs purchase price, multi-tenant vs single-tenant caps).
  Ex: "Multi-tenant industrial trades at tighter cap rates than single-tenant
  build-to-suit on the same dirt — we bring that logic to powered land."

**9. Lead with a recurring one-line thesis and repeat it verbatim across the deck.**
  Ex: "Powered land is bottlenecked by power and permitting. Obsidian solves both.
  Various execution modes, same thesis."

**10. Bold only the load-bearing phrase or number inside an otherwise plain
sentence — never decorate.** One or two bolds per sentence, max.
  Ex: "The asset also benefits from a **~90% reduced tax burden** due to the ICAP
  tax abatement (24 yrs. remaining)."

**11. Compress causal chains with arrows / colons / equals signs when the logic is
sequential.**
  Ex: "Demand is accelerating → physical capacity is sold out → Europe is
  structurally short → cap rate spreads are widening." "DERMS expertise + ComEd
  sites + IPA eligibility = first-mover advantage."

**12. Quarantine legal hedging in the disclaimer; keep the body confident.** All
"no representation or warranty… subject to material variation" boilerplate lives
on the Confidentiality/Disclaimer slide. The body never hedges the thesis. Define
terms once in quotes-and-parens — ("Sponsor"), ("Agent") — then reuse.

**13. Titles: clean noun label with the assertion in the bold sub-headline (OM
style), OR a full-sentence thesis title (platform/teaser style).** Use an
em-dash/colon to bolt a thesis onto a label.
  Ex (label + subhead): "Asset Location" → "105 North 13th Street is located at
  the intersection of prime Williamsburg and Greenpoint."
  Ex (thesis title): "PowerHouse Joliet — A 1.33 GW Campus Stuck on Customer
  Search."

**Choosing the title register:** single-asset OMs default to noun-label titles
with assertion sub-heads (register A); programmatic/platform decks default to
full-sentence thesis titles (register B). Match the deck type. (NPV IOS is a
programmatic raise → lean register B for the strategy slides, register A for the
deal-exhibit and financial slides.)

PART 2 — THE ANTI-AI-TELL RULESET (run as a final pass over every slide)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Ordered by how loudly the tell screams "AI." The behavioral rules (name a number,
take a side, vary rhythm, read it aloud) are durable; the banned-word list ages as
models adapt, so treat words as examples of a manner to avoid, not a fixed
blocklist. **Do not write to beat a detector — detectors are unreliable. Write to
be specific, and the tells disappear.**

**1. Kill the negation antithesis.** Banned: "It's not just X — it's Y." / "Not
only… but also." Instead: state the claim once, directly, with evidence.
  Before: "This isn't just a warehouse — it's a logistics nerve center."
  After: "FedEx leases 60% of the building through 2031 and runs three same-day
  routes out of it."

**2. Demand a number, name, or date in every claim.** Banned: unsupported
adjectives ("well-positioned," "strong fundamentals," "compelling," "best-in-class,"
"institutional-quality," "irreplaceable," "trophy"). Instead: cash out every
qualitative claim in a figure, proper noun, or date.
  Before: "The asset boasts strong fundamentals."
  After: "Occupancy has held above 94% since 2019; in-place rents trail market 11%."

**3. Purge the top-tier AI vocabulary.** Empirically the loudest (Kobak et al.,
Science Advances 2025: "delves" 28×, "underscores" 14×, "showcasing" 11×):
delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, crucial, robust, intricate, tapestry,
testament, landscape (figurative), realm, foster, leverage (verb), boast,
seamless, unlock, elevate, ever-evolving, game-changer, navigate (figurative).
Instead: the plainest exact verb/noun.
  Before: "This underscores the asset's robust, ever-evolving appeal."
  After: "Re-leasing spreads of 8–12% show pricing power."

**4. Restore "is."** Banned: copula-dodging — "stands as / serves as a testament
to / represents / boasts." Instead: "is / are / has."
  Before: "The property stands as a testament to infill scarcity."
  After: "There are no developable infill parcels left within two miles."

**5. Take a side; state a thesis.** Banned: both-sides hedging and vague
"bigger-picture" wrap-ups with no view. Instead: lead with the recommendation and
its two or three reasons. (This is also House Voice rule 1.)
  Before: "There are many factors investors may wish to consider."
  After: "Underwrite this to a 6.5% exit, not 6.0% — here's why."

**6. Vary sentence length on purpose.** Banned: paragraphs of uniformly medium
sentences. Instead: let a 4–6 word sentence land after a 25+ word one. (House
Voice rule 2.)

**7. One em-dash per slide, maximum.** The "—" tell is overstated but density
still reads as AI. Reserve it for one genuine interruption; the rest become
periods or colons.

**8. Break the tricolon habit.** Banned: reflexive "faster, smarter, and more
efficient" triads. Instead: name one or two concrete things, or vary the count.
  Before: "Modern, efficient, and well-located."
  After: "Built 2021; 32-foot clears; one turn to I-4."

**9. Cut the restating summary sentence and the "In conclusion / Overall / In
summary" wrap.** End on the strongest specific fact or the explicit ask.
  Before: "Overall, this is a compelling opportunity."
  After: "First-round bids are due March 14."

**10. Ban hedge stacks.** Banned: "may potentially help to contribute to." Instead:
one calibrated modal + a specific magnitude.
  Before: "This could potentially help drive meaningful upside."
  After: "Marking rents to market adds roughly $1.4M of NOI."

**11. Vary bullet structure.** Banned: every bullet the same length and grammar
(all gerunds, all one line). Instead: mix fragments and clauses; lead with the
number.
  Before: five gerund bullets. After: "$42 PSF in-place. / Market is $47 (CBRE
  Q4). / Three leases roll in 2026 — the mark-to-market."

**12. Drop the "challenges & future outlook" boilerplate.** Banned: "Despite its
strengths, the asset faces challenges… Future Outlook." Instead: a real risk
register with magnitude and mitigant. (House Voice rule 6.)
  After: "Risk: 180k SF of new supply delivers 2027. Mitigant: it's big-box; our
  suites are 20–40k SF — a different tenant pool."

**13. Repeat the precise term; don't elegantly vary.** Banned: "the asset / the
property / this offering / the investment" cycling in four lines. Instead: pick
the right noun and reuse it.

**14. Break connective chains.** Banned: consecutive sentences opening with
Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally. Instead: start with the subject; let logic
carry the link.

**15. Strip relentless positivity; concede something real.** An OM with zero
acknowledged risk reads as a snow-job to a skeptical LP. Name the genuine weakness
they'll find anyway, and address it. (House Voice rule 6.)

**16. Headers are labels, not sentences; no full-sentence title case.** (Reconcile
with House Voice rule 13: noun-label titles carry the assertion in the bold
sub-headline; full-sentence thesis titles are a deliberate register choice, not
the AI tell of capitalizing a whole sentence as a header.)

**17. No emoji, no 🚀, sparing bold.** Zero emoji. Bold at most one number per
slide. (House Voice rule 10.)

**18. Pass the spoken-language test (Paul Graham).** Banned: any sentence you'd
never say aloud to a colleague — fancy prose that hides empty content. Rewrite it
the way you'd actually say it.
  Before: "This opportunity is poised to unlock significant value-creation
  potential." After: "We think there's about $6M of upside, mostly from
  re-leasing."

**19. CRE-specific puffery is double-banned.** "well-positioned," "strong
fundamentals," "compelling opportunity," "mission-critical," "durable cash flow,"
"supply-constrained," "value-add" used as an adjective rather than a described
plan — these are BOTH generic marketing-speak AND exactly what a trained LP
mentally deletes. Keep the claim, force the number behind it.

**20. Final read-aloud.** Read the slide aloud. If it sounds metronomic, smooth,
and content-free, it's failing. Fix the flattest sentence by adding a specific or
changing its length. This catch-all surfaces tells the other 19 miss.

SOURCES (for the anti-AI ruleset)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025 (PMC12219543) — empirical word-frequency
  spike: delves 28×, underscores 14×, showcasing 11×; 2024 spike = 66% verbs.
- Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing" — editor-maintained field guide (negation,
  copula avoidance, tricolon, elegant variation, boldface/emoji/title-case tells).
- Paul Graham, "Write Simply" — fancy writing conceals the lack of ideas; the
  spoken-language test.

  positivity, vague confident adjectives, no point of view.
Caveat: em-dash and curly-quote tells are weak/context-dependent — don't
over-index. The durable rules are behavioral, not the blocklist.
