# Anti-AI-Tell Ruleset

Ordered by how loudly the tell screams "AI." The behavioral rules (name a number, take a side, vary rhythm, read it aloud) are durable; the banned-word list ages as models adapt, so treat words as examples of a manner to avoid, not a fixed blocklist. Do not write to beat a detector — detectors are unreliable. Write to be specific, and the tells disappear.

---

## PART 1 — CORE ANTI-AI RULES (ported from house-voice.md Part 2)

**1. Kill the negation antithesis.** Banned: "It's not just X — it's Y." / "Not only… but also." Instead: state the claim once, directly, with evidence.
Before: "This isn't just a warehouse — it's a logistics nerve center."
After: "FedEx leases 60% of the building through 2031 and runs three same-day routes out of it."

**2. Demand a number, name, or date in every claim.** Banned unsupported adjectives: "well-positioned," "strong fundamentals," "compelling," "best-in-class," "institutional-quality," "irreplaceable," "trophy." Cash out every qualitative claim in a figure, proper noun, or date.
Before: "The asset boasts strong fundamentals."
After: "Occupancy has held above 94% since 2019; in-place rents trail market 11%."

**3. Purge the top-tier AI vocabulary.** Empirically the loudest (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025: "delves" 28×, "underscores" 14×, "showcasing" 11×): delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, crucial, robust, intricate, tapestry, testament, landscape (figurative), realm, foster, leverage (verb), boast, seamless, unlock, elevate, ever-evolving, game-changer, navigate (figurative). Use the plainest exact verb or noun instead.
Before: "This underscores the asset's robust, ever-evolving appeal."
After: "Re-leasing spreads of 8–12% show pricing power."

**4. Restore "is."** Banned copula-dodging: "stands as / serves as a testament to / represents / boasts." Use "is / are / has."
Before: "The property stands as a testament to infill scarcity."
After: "There are no developable infill parcels left within two miles."

**5. Take a side; state a thesis.** Banned: both-sides hedging and vague "bigger-picture" wrap-ups with no view. Lead with the recommendation and its two or three reasons.
Before: "There are many factors investors may wish to consider."
After: "Underwrite this to a 6.5% exit, not 6.0% — here's why."

**6. Vary sentence length on purpose.** Banned: paragraphs of uniformly medium sentences. Let a 4–6-word sentence land after a 25+-word one.

**7. One em-dash per slide, maximum.** Reserve it for one genuine interruption; the rest become periods or colons.

**8. Break the tricolon habit.** Banned: reflexive three-item triads ("faster, smarter, and more efficient"). Name one or two concrete things, or vary the count.
Before: "Modern, efficient, and well-located."
After: "Built 2021; 32-foot clears; one turn to I-4."

**9. Cut the restating summary sentence and the "In conclusion / Overall / In summary" wrap.** End on the strongest specific fact or the explicit ask.
Before: "Overall, this is a compelling opportunity."
After: "First-round bids are due March 14."

**10. Ban hedge stacks.** Banned: "may potentially help to contribute to." One calibrated modal + a specific magnitude.
Before: "This could potentially help drive meaningful upside."
After: "Marking rents to market adds roughly $1.4M of NOI."

**11. Vary bullet structure.** Banned: every bullet the same length and grammar (all gerunds, all one line). Mix fragments and clauses; lead with the number.
Before: five gerund bullets.
After: "$42 PSF in-place. / Market is $47 (CBRE Q4). / Three leases roll in 2026 — the mark-to-market."

**12. Drop the "challenges & future outlook" boilerplate.** Banned: "Despite its strengths, the asset faces challenges… Future Outlook." Instead: a real risk register with magnitude and mitigant.
After: "Risk: 180k SF of new supply delivers 2027. Mitigant: it's big-box; our suites are 20–40k SF — a different tenant pool."

**13. Repeat the precise term; don't elegantly vary.** Banned: "the asset / the property / this offering / the investment" cycling in four lines. Pick the right noun and reuse it.

**14. Break connective chains.** Banned: consecutive sentences opening with "Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally." Start with the subject; let logic carry the link.

**15. Strip relentless positivity; concede something real.** An OM with zero acknowledged risk reads as a snow-job to a skeptical LP. Name the genuine weakness they will find anyway, and address it.

**16. Headers are labels, not sentences; no full-sentence title case.** (Reconcile with deck platform register: full-sentence thesis titles — "Powered land is bottlenecked by power and permitting. Obsidian solves both." — are a deliberate register choice for platform/GP decks, not the AI tell of capitalizing a whole sentence as a standard header.)

**17. No emoji, no decorative icons, sparing bold.** Zero emoji. Bold at most one number per slide. In investment highlight bullets: bold the label only, not the clause.

**18. Pass the spoken-language test (Paul Graham).** Banned: any sentence you would never say aloud to a colleague. Rewrite it the way you would actually say it.
Before: "This opportunity is poised to unlock significant value-creation potential."
After: "We think there's about $6M of upside, mostly from re-leasing."

**19. CRE-specific puffery is double-banned.** "well-positioned," "strong fundamentals," "compelling opportunity," "mission-critical," "durable cash flow," "supply-constrained," "value-add" used as an adjective rather than a described plan — these are both generic marketing-speak AND exactly what a trained LP mentally deletes. Keep the claim; force the number behind it.

**20. Final read-aloud.** Read the slide aloud. If it sounds metronomic, smooth, and content-free, it is failing. Fix the flattest sentence by adding a specific or changing its length. This catch-all surfaces tells the other 19 miss.

### Sources (for Part 1)
- Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025 (PMC12219543) — empirical word-frequency spike: delves 28×, underscores 14×, showcasing 11×; 2024 spike = 66% verbs.
- Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing" — editor-maintained field guide (negation, copula avoidance, tricolon, elegant variation, boldface/emoji/title-case tells).
- Paul Graham, "Write Simply" — fancy writing conceals the lack of ideas; the spoken-language test.
- Stack Overflow "The AI ick" / Charlie Guo "Field Guide to AI Slop" — relentless positivity, vague confident adjectives, no point of view.

Caveat: em-dash and curly-quote tells are weak/context-dependent — do not over-index. The durable rules are behavioral, not the blocklist.

## PART 2 — OBSERVED V23 NEGATIVE SPACE (from the 2026-06 pattern study)

These are patterns conspicuously absent from all investor-facing V23 materials, derived from observed absence across the corpus — not from generic AI-tell lists.

**N-01 — No "Translation:" or "Bottom line:" in any investor document.**
Found in 6 internal memos (G series, 2024–2026). Zero occurrences in any of the 30+ investor-facing OMs reviewed across Slices B, C, D, E, F. The dividing line is sharp and consistent. These markers function as an authenticity signal for internal use and as a disqualifying tell in investor-facing drafts.

**N-02 — No per-slide inline source lines in final investor deck OMs.**
NPV OM v0 (5/26) had per-slide source blocks; stripped by 6/01 and absent from the final. 105 N13 final has no source lines on any slide. All sourcing compresses to parenthetical superscripts on the exec summary stat box only. (Exception: the V23 dark Krios format retains 8–8.5pt steel-gray source lines at the bottom of each market-data slide — a Krios/Everleaf format-specific convention, not V23 Garamond standard.)

**N-03 — No "attractiveness rating" scores in investor materials.**
The X/10 rating appears in 4 internal memos (Blue Harbor, View on Detroit East, The Crossing, others in Slice G). Never in any investor-facing document across the entire corpus.

**N-04 — No blended aggregate IRR in platform-mode investor OMs.**
The Build Blueprint explicitly prohibits it; the NPV v0's "27% blended IRR" was removed by v3 and permanently excluded from the final. The Obsidian platform deck carries no platform-level return target anywhere. The Krios/Everleaf teaser does not aggregate return across sites.

**N-05 — No icons, emojis, or decorative bullets in any V23 investor document.**
Across all deck OMs (105 N13, NPV IOS, Obsidian, Krios Everleaf, Alley North), bullets are typographic dashes or plain dots. No checkmarks, icons, or emoji found in any shape or text run. Not in Word OMs either.

**N-06 — No em-dash chains (X — Y — Z — W) in investor prose.**
Em-dash is used in the bold-label — evidence-clause Investment Highlights pattern (single dash per bullet). Not used as a multi-break stylistic device within prose sentences.

**N-07 — No first-person "I/we" in investor-facing OMs.**
All investor OMs use "Vanadium" / "the Agent" / "the Sponsor" in third person throughout. First-person voice appears only in: Strug raw meeting notes (Slice A, internal), some internal Slack-voice memos in G series, and Obsidian Nov 2025 WIP drafts before they reached investor-ready state.

**N-08 — No risk section in the deck body of a standard investor OM.**
105 N13, NPV IOS, and all non-institutional V23 deck OMs have no named "Risk Factors" or "Risk & Mitigants" section. Risk is surfaced via: (a) explicit downside protection metric in Investment Highlights, (b) companion memo quarantine (Durability Memo, Industrial Usage Memo), (c) the "alternative use / floor value" framing. A standalone risk section appears only in the institutional full OM (Krios v5, Slice C), which is the most formal institutional register in the corpus.

**N-09 — No deal returns in outreach email/memo prose beyond the headline 4-KPI bar.**
In outreach memos (Slice G Varsity, Chickering, Piedmont Triad), deal financials appear as a 4-cell table at the top, then the body is narrative. No additional return metrics are embedded in the prose body. The 4 KPIs (price / equity raise / IRR / EM) are the ceiling for numeric density in an outreach memo.

**N-10 — No "exciting opportunity" or content-free promotional sentences.**
In investor-facing materials, the opening sentence always names parties, asset, and capital type — never an unprompted assertion of attractiveness. Internal memos are the only place where unqualified evaluative adjectives appear.

## PART 3 — BLIND-JUDGE TELLS (2026-06 reproduction test)

Distilled from the round-1 defect diagnosis. Four patterns that caused all three judges to correctly identify generated passages as imitations.

**Tell 1 — Over-completion.** Generated prose covered every standard OM category in textbook sequence. Every clause closed. Every dual thesis restated. Real prose tolerates gaps, asymmetry, and roughness ("6 bridge loans and a combination of various missteps and prolonged delays"). Diagnostic question: does this block need to close with that sentence, or does the evidence speak for itself?

**Tell 2 — Verdict clauses in highlight bullets.** Real highlight bullets are clipped and withhold explanation. Generated bullets closed with "...confirming both execution capability and X" or "...demonstrating that Y is achievable." Remove any bullet-closing clause that names the conclusion the bullet already implies.

**Tell 3 — Floating round numbers.** Generated figures were round and impressive without source anchors ("14.3% annual rent growth," "8.2M SF absorption"). Real figures are odd-precision model fallout ($13,462/unit, 8.19%, 45.6%) and/or carry named source anchors. If the figure is round and has no source, it is a suspect.

**Tell 4 — Self-explaining bullets.** Bullets that explain why the evidence matters signal AI authorship to a trained judge. "Sponsor completed 315 Meserole — demonstrating its ability to execute the office-to-industrial conversion strategy" is a self-explaining bullet. "315 Meserole: 38k SF leased in 10 months, conversion complete" is not.

**Round-2 residual tell (1 survivor after GP-rules applied):** Descriptive asset-overview blocks with no protection calculus — "stylistically inert relative to authenticated passages, which always embed a defensive numeric." Every block, including location, sponsor background, and asset description blocks, carries at least one defensive numeric or anchored comparison before leaving draft. (See voice-model.md GP-5.)
